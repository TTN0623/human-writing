# Internal Report: September Website Conversion Rate Decline

**Prepared by:** Digital Analytics Team
**Date:** October 2026
**Audience:** Marketing and Product Leadership

## Executive Summary

Our website conversion rate declined from 3.2% in August to 2.4% in September, a drop of roughly 25%. This report outlines the key factors identified and recommends next steps to recover performance.

## Key Findings

### 1. Shift in Traffic Mix
In September, paid social traffic increased significantly following the back-to-school campaign. While this drove higher visitor volume, these users showed lower purchase intent and converted at nearly half the rate of organic and email visitors. The larger share of low-intent traffic diluted the overall conversion rate.

### 2. Checkout Page Performance Issues
Following the site update on September 9, the average checkout page load time increased from 2.1 to 3.8 seconds on mobile devices. Cart abandonment on mobile rose by 12 percentage points during this period, suggesting that slower performance contributed directly to lost sales.

### 3. Pricing and Shipping Changes
The introduction of a new free-shipping threshold ($75, up from $50) on September 15 appears to have discouraged smaller orders. Exit surveys indicate that unexpected shipping costs were the most frequently cited reason for abandonment.

### 4. Seasonal Factors
September typically sees a modest dip in conversions as customers shift from summer purchases to budget-conscious behavior. However, this factor accounts for only an estimated 3 to 5 percent of the decline.

## Recommendations

1. **Optimize mobile checkout speed** by compressing scripts and images, with a target load time under 2.5 seconds.
2. **Refine paid social targeting** to prioritize high-intent audiences and retargeting segments.
3. **Test the shipping threshold** through an A/B experiment before making it permanent.
4. **Monitor weekly metrics** to track recovery progress.

## Conclusion

The decline results from a combination of traffic quality, technical performance, and policy changes. Addressing these areas should help restore conversion rates to previous levels by the end of Q4.
